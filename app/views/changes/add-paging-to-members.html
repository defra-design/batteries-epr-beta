Show 30 members at a time on the Members page (EB-366)

Change

The Members page now shows 30 members at a time, with links to the other pages underneath. Before, all members were on one page. Recycling Lives has 281 members, so this meant a lot of scrolling. A keyboard user would also have had to press Tab more than 560 times to reach the end, because each row has a tickbox and a link.

Why 30

Alex's registration list shows 20 at a time. We chose 30 because it is long enough to scan without changing page too often, but still quick to move through with a keyboard. We can change this if research shows regulators want more or fewer.

Sorting works across the whole list

Phil has confirmed that the system can sort long lists and split them into pages itself, on the server. This means sorting puts the whole list in order, not just the members on the current page. The sortable table we used before could only sort what was on the page, so it no longer worked.

Each column heading is a link. Selecting it sorts the list, and selecting it again reverses the order. This works the same way as the sortable column heading in Defra Internal Frontend, Geoff's library of shared components. The list starts with the members whose figures changed most.

The sort order and page number are in the web address. This means the back button, bookmarks and shared links all work, and the page works without JavaScript.

Searching and selecting members

Search and the membership filter look through all members, not just the current page, and take you back to page 1.

Members you select stay selected when you move between pages. Without JavaScript, your selections are saved when you select Continue to make a decision, so you need to do this before changing page.

There is no option to select all members, so paging does not affect that. The Flags page is not split into pages, because there are fewer than 30 flags.

Questions for research

  • Is 30 members a page the right number?
  • Do regulators sort by anything other than the largest change?
